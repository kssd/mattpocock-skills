I started writing at 6am. The cursor blinked. Nothing came.

Three months earlier I had quit my job to write full-time. My savings runway was eight months. I had a draft outline and a head full of ideas. But the blank page had its own opinion.

The thing nobody tells you about quitting to write: you also quit the structure. The 9am standup. The Slack ping at 2pm. The reason to stop. Without those rails, time turns to water.

I tried timers. I tried playlists. I tried writing in cafes, in libraries, in the car parked outside the house because it felt less like home and more like somewhere else.

What eventually worked was the opposite of discipline. It was giving myself permission to write badly. One sentence. Then another. Not for an audience. For the pile.

By month two the pile was 40,000 words of fragments, half-arguments, scenes, contradictions. None of it was an article. All of it was material.

Now I know: the blank page isn't where writing starts. The pile is where writing starts. The blank page is just where you go once you have something to quarry.
